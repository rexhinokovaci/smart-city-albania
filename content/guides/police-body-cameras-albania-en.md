**Body-worn cameras** are devices that police officers wear on their uniform and that record video and audio during duty. Unlike fixed cameras, they move with the officer and can also record private spaces, such as a home or a vehicle. This guide explains what is publicly known and which standards apply. It does not assess any specific case.

## Are body cameras part of Smart City?

According to parliamentary documents cited by the media, the Smart City programme includes a body-camera component for the State Police. Read the sourced summary in [what we know about Smart City](../../lajme/smart-city-albania-what-we-know/). Reported figures differ between sources, so we do not treat them as established. According to the statements cited there, this component is still under development.

## How are they different from street cameras?

- **They move.** They can enter private premises or places where people expect less intrusion.
- **They record sound.** Audio also captures data of third parties.
- **A person activates them.** When they switch on, and who decides, is a key question.
- **They can capture people in sensitive situations,** such as victims, witnesses or minors.

## Which standards apply?

- **Law no. 124/2024** on personal data protection, which aligns Albanian rules with those of the EU.
- **Directive (EU) 2016/680**, which sets the rules for data processing by police: specified purpose, necessity, proportionality, limited storage and security.
- **GDPR principles:** minimisation, accuracy, storage limitation, accountability.
- **Article 8 of the European Convention on Human Rights (ECHR):** any interference with private life needs a legal basis, a legitimate aim and must be necessary in a democratic society.

For the general framework, read [camera surveillance and EU standards](../camera-surveillance-and-eu-standards-en/).

## Questions without a public answer

Based on the information we have found, these points are not publicly documented. We do not claim they are missing inside the institutions, only that we have not found them published:

1. When must the camera be switched on, and who checks that this is respected?
2. How long are recordings kept, and when are they deleted?
3. Who has access, and is every search logged?
4. Has a data protection impact assessment (DPIA) been published?
5. Does the vendor have access to recordings or to the storage platform?
6. Is facial recognition used on the video? See the [EU AI Act and facial recognition](../njohja-e-fytyres-dhe-akti-i-be-per-ia/).
7. What extra protection exists for minors and victims?
8. How are people informed that they are being recorded?

## What you can do as a citizen

- **Request public information** about usage rules: [how to file a right-to-information request](../how-to-file-a-right-to-information-request-about-cameras-en/).
- **Request your own data** if you think you were recorded: [how to request camera footage of yourself](../how-to-request-camera-footage-of-yourself-en/).
- **Complain to the IDP Commissioner** if you get no answer: [how to complain to the IDP Commissioner](../how-to-complain-to-the-idp-commissioner-about-cameras-en/).

Legal questions are decided by the Commissioner for the Right to Information and Data Protection and by the courts. Our aim is for these questions to be clear and to receive public answers. See also the [camera map](../../).
