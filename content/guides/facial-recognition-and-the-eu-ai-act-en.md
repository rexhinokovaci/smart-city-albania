AI-enabled cameras raise a different question from ordinary cameras: can they **recognise people by their face**? This guide explains the European standard and the questions that remain open. It does not claim that any institution has acted unlawfully.

## What is facial recognition?

Facial recognition is a type of **biometric identification**: the system compares facial features with a database to say who a person is. It differs from a camera that simply records footage, and from a plate reader ([see ANPR](../license-plate-reader-cameras-alpr-anpr-en/)) that reads a vehicle number.

European standards treat biometric data as a sensitive category, because a face cannot be changed like a password.

## What does the EU AI Act say?

Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, known as the **EU AI Act**, in principle prohibits **real-time remote biometric identification** in publicly accessible spaces for law-enforcement purposes. Only narrow exceptions listed in the Act are allowed, generally with prior authorisation.

The Act also prohibits building facial-recognition databases by untargeted scraping of images from the internet or from surveillance cameras.

## Does the Act apply in Albania?

Albania is not an EU member, so the Act does not apply directly. As a candidate country, however, it is aligning its legislation with the EU, and the Act is the benchmark against which European practices are compared. Personal data is governed by **Law No. 124/2024**, built on the GDPR and Directive (EU) 2016/680. See also [EU standards on surveillance](../camera-surveillance-and-eu-standards-en/).

## What has been said publicly about Smart City?

Public sources describe the Smart City programme as a system of intelligent cameras, plate readers and police body cameras. This guide does not state that the system does or does not use facial recognition: we have not found a public document that settles the question.

## Questions without a public answer

1. Does the system include **facial-recognition** functions, or could they be switched on later?
2. If so, on what **legal basis** and with what prior authorisation?
3. Has a **data protection impact assessment (DPIA)** been published?
4. What is the **retention period** for footage and biometric data?
5. Who has **access**, including the vendor and third parties, and are searches logged?
6. What extra protection exists for **children**, especially in schools?
7. Are there **signs** near the cameras?

## How can you ask the institutions?

- File a [right-to-information request](../how-to-file-a-right-to-information-request-about-cameras-en/) under Law No. 119/2014.
- If you have a concern about your own data, [complain to the IDP Commissioner](../how-to-complain-to-the-idp-commissioner-about-cameras-en/).
- Browse the [camera map](../../) and, for schools, see [parents' questions](../cameras-in-schools-parents-questions-en/).

Whether a practice complies with the law is for the Commissioner and the courts to determine.
