Besides the public **Smart City** cameras, many apartment buildings, shops and houses have their own private cameras. They often film an entrance, a yard or part of the pavement. This guide explains the general European principles that apply to them. It is not legal advice.

> This guide presents standards and questions. It does not claim that any person or institution has broken the law. Findings of that kind belong to the IDP Commissioner and the courts.

## Can I install a camera in my building or house?

Albania has aligned its data protection rules with the EU through **Law No. 124/2024**, which follows GDPR principles. The general principle is the same: a camera that films people processes personal data. It should therefore have a clear purpose (for example protecting property) and should not film more than is necessary.

## What is the European standard for cameras that film the street?

According to European Data Protection Board (EDPB) guidelines on video devices, and the case law of the Court of Justice of the EU (the Ryneš case), the "household exemption" usually does **not** cover a camera that films a public space such as a road or pavement. In such cases the camera owner is treated as responsible for the data.

In practice, a camera should point mainly at the owner's own property, not at neighbours' doors or the street.

## Which principles apply to any private camera?

- **Defined purpose**: what the camera is for, and why a less intrusive measure would not be enough.
- **Minimisation**: film only the necessary areas, and avoid places where people expect privacy, such as balconies and windows.
- **Signage**: a visible sign saying the area is filmed and who is responsible.
- **Limited retention**: footage is deleted when no longer needed, not kept indefinitely.
- **Limited access**: only named people can view it, not the whole neighbourhood or social media groups.
- **Security**: strong passwords and no open connections to the internet.

## What can I do if a camera films me?

1. Ask the owner or building administrator what the camera is for and how long footage is kept.
2. If you think it necessary, ask in writing for the camera to be redirected or the relevant area masked.
3. Ask for a copy of footage in which you appear. See [how to request camera footage of yourself](../how-to-request-camera-footage-of-yourself-en/).
4. If you get no answer, you may turn to the Commissioner. See [how to complain to the IDP Commissioner](../how-to-complain-to-the-idp-commissioner-about-cameras-en/).

Avoid posting camera footage on social media: it can contain faces, number plates and children.

## How do private cameras relate to public ones?

Private cameras are not part of the Smart City programme. In some countries authorities may ask owners for footage in a specific case. What rules apply to such requests in Albania is a question that deserves a public explanation. For public cameras, read [what is publicly known about Smart City](../smart-city-albania-what-we-know/), [EU standards on surveillance](../camera-surveillance-and-eu-standards-en/) and [licence plate reader cameras](../license-plate-reader-cameras-alpr-anpr-en/).
