Law No. 119/2014 "On the right to information" lets anyone request documents and information from public authorities. This also applies to cameras: for example the Ministry of Interior, the State Police or a municipality.

> This guide is informational and is not legal advice. Check the current text of the law (amended by Law No. 78/2023) on the Commissioner's website or in the Official Gazette.

The Albanian version is [Si të bësh kërkesë për informim për kamerat](../si-te-besh-kerkese-per-informim-per-kamerat/).

## What can you ask about cameras?

Ask for documents and data that exist, not for opinions. Some examples:

- A list of public camera locations in a municipality or area.
- The data protection impact assessment (DPIA), if one was prepared.
- How long footage is kept and who has access to it.
- The contract or decision governing the project, to the extent it is not classified.
- Information on where information signs are placed.

Also read the [open questions that remain publicly unanswered](../standardet-e-be-per-mbikeqyrjen/) about surveillance cameras.

## How to write the request

1. Identify the public authority precisely (for example the Ministry of Interior, the State Police or the municipality).
2. Describe clearly the document or information you want.
3. Say how you want the answer (email or paper copy) and give your contact details.
4. Refer explicitly to Law No. 119/2014 "On the right to information".

Keep proof of sending and the date. You will need it if you complain later.

## How long does the authority have to answer?

According to the text of the law, as cited in public official materials, a request is handled as soon as possible and **no later than 10 working days** from submission, unless a special law provides otherwise. If the authority does not hold the information, it must forward the request to the competent authority.

## Where do you complain if there is no answer?

An administrative complaint goes to the Commissioner for the Right to Information and Protection of Personal Data (IDP), within **30 working days** of receiving the refusal or of the expiry of the legal deadline for a reply. The complaint should name the authority, say what happened and state what you ask of the Commissioner, with documents attached.

Unlike a request to see footage in which you appear, which is based on Law 124/2024, this route is for public information. For the other case, read [how to request camera footage of yourself](../how-to-request-camera-footage-of-yourself-en/). To complain about data protection, see [how to complain to the IDP Commissioner](../how-to-complain-to-the-idp-commissioner-about-cameras-en/).

## Why does it matter?

Documented requests help keep the discussion about Smart City grounded in facts. Institutions can answer publicly, and citizens can compare the answers with EU standards. You can also use the [map of known cameras](../../) to find the area you are interested in.

## FAQ

**Do I have to explain why I want the information?** Do not include unnecessary personal data in the request. Check the text of the law for the rules on justification and any copying costs.

**Can I request camera footage with this law?** No. Footage showing identifiable people is personal data and is handled under Law 124/2024.

**Does a request guarantee a full answer?** No. Some information may be restricted for reasons set out in law, but a refusal must be notified and can be appealed.
